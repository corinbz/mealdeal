mealdeal

Your shopping list, priced at every chain that can actually fill it.

Dutch shelf labels already carry a unit price — buried in grey small print beneath the headline deal. That small print is the only number that compares. So it is the largest figure in every cell below, and the pack price is the footnote.

Quantity, unit, then what it is. Without a unit, the page guesses one from what the chains sell.

Every price here is real, read from the latest Sweep of each chain (dated above). Names, pack sizes, prices and offer wording are as the chains sent them — nothing is simulated, and nothing is derived from an offer's wording. Rebuild with node scripts/catalogue-from-db.ts after an ingest.

Unit prices come from each chain where it publishes its own, and are derived from pack size where it does not.

A list item is a requirement, not a product — each chain resolves it against its own assortment, picking the lowest unit price among products whose name contains every word you typed. That is crude, and some rows will be wrong. Click a price to correct it; the correction is remembered for next time.

A staple stays on the list between trips. Everything else is a one-off, cleared by Done shopping. The list lives in this browser only.

Offer wording is shown exactly as the chain wrote it. Nothing here converts 1+1 gratis into a percentage, because the true effective price depends on how many you buy.

Corrections are saved in this browser and applied whenever this item is on the list.
