One basket, priced at every chain that can actually fill it.

Dutch shelf labels already carry a unit price — buried in grey small print beneath the headline deal. That small print is the only number that compares. So it is the largest figure in every cell below, and the pack price is the footnote. Twelve requirements, resolved against 24,463 real products.

Every price here is real. Both chains were swept on 2026-10-13 and ingested: 7,359 Dirk products from store 66, 17,104 from Jumbo nationally. Names, pack sizes, prices and offer wording are as the chains sent them — nothing on this page is simulated, and nothing is derived from an offer's wording.

Unit prices come from each chain where it publishes its own, and are derived from pack size where it does not. Dirk publishes none, so all of its figures are derived; Jumbo publishes its own for 16,938 of its products, and those are the ones shown.

A basket item is a requirement, not a product — each chain resolves it against its own assortment. Resolution picks the lowest unit price among products sharing a comparison unit, which is why several rows below are visibly wrong. Correcting one is the point, not a workaround.

Offer wording is shown exactly as the chain wrote it. Nothing here converts 1+1 gratis into a percentage, because the true effective price depends on how many you buy.

Corrections are reviewed and folded back into the shared vocabulary — they do not stay private to one shopper.
