One basket, priced at every chain that can actually fill it.

Dutch shelf labels already carry a unit price — buried in grey small print beneath the headline deal. That small print is the only number that compares. So it is the largest figure in every cell below, and the pack price is the footnote.

Dirk prices are real. Product names, pack sizes, shelf prices and offer wording come from archive/2026-10-13/dirk — one store, one morning, recorded as the chain sent it. Jumbo prices are invented and every one is marked simulated: Jumbo's endpoint refuses an honest client, so there is nothing real to show yet.

A basket item is a requirement, not a product — each chain resolves it against its own assortment. Resolution picks the lowest unit price among products sharing a comparison unit, which is why several rows below are visibly wrong. Correcting one is the point, not a workaround.

Offer wording is shown exactly as the chain wrote it. Nothing here converts 1+1 gratis into a percentage, because the true effective price depends on how many you buy.

Corrections are reviewed and folded back into the shared vocabulary — they do not stay private to one shopper.
